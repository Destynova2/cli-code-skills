# B2B Sales: Discovery, Qualification, Deal Reviews

Use when the user sells (services, audit, platform, freelance offer) and the
problem is upstream of price: unclear need, no identified buyer, a free POC
request, or coaching a salesperson. Settle scope and decision process before
negotiating the number; price talks with no decision-maker in the room only
set a floor for the next round.

## Discovery (SPIN Selling)

Start from what is already known. Ask the question that teaches something,
then build on the answer. Fictional example, an AI model access service:

| Intent | Possible question |
|---|---|
| Useful situation | « Par où passe une demande d'accès à un nouveau modèle aujourd'hui ? » |
| Problem lived | « À quel moment la dernière demande a bloqué ? » |
| Consequence | « Qu'est-ce que l'équipe a fait pendant cette attente ? » |
| Value as the client sees it | « Si ce blocage disparaissait, qu'est-ce que votre équipe livrerait plus tôt ? » |

Ask for a precise example, a frequency, or an order of magnitude when the
client can give one. Restate the problem in their words, then demo that case.
If the problem is minor, say a sale may not be justified.

## Backing a Claim (Feature, Advantage, Proof)

One relevant feature, the concrete benefit for this client, and a proof the
client can inspect. Example for an audit service: if the deliverable includes
a review workshop, the operations lead can get fixes arbitrated with the teams
concerned there. Show an agenda and an anonymised sample report if one exists.
A proof still to be produced is presented as such. Ask the client whether it
meets their criterion.

## Qualifying a Complex Deal (MEDDPICC)

Private prep only. For each line: a fact, its source, or the next action that
verifies it. Report one or two decisive gaps to the user, not the full grid.

| To establish | Verifiable signal |
|---|---|
| Metric and priority pain | The client describes an incident, its impact, and the success criterion. |
| Economic buyer and champion | You know who can commit the budget. A real champion has already obtained an arbitration, information, or a useful meeting. |
| Decision criteria and process | The client explains how options will be compared, by whom, at which step. |
| Paper process | Procurement, legal, security, and signature steps are known, in order when order matters. |
| Competition | Internal tool, status quo, and other projects competing for the same budget. |

An enthusiastic contact may just be an interested user. A successful technical
test may still lack a budget owner. If the prospect asks for a free POC, first
check the uncertainty it removes, who runs the test, what each side commits,
and the decision planned after the result. Then weigh cost against interest.

Use *The Challenger Sale* only with a reliable observation that can change the
client's diagnosis: ask permission, present the fact and its possible
implication, invite them to challenge the reading. With nothing verifiable, go
back to discovery.

Close on a mutual action with owner and date:

> « Si c'est bien le circuit d'autorisation qui bloque, qui pourrait nous
> montrer un dossier réel au prochain échange ? »

## Coaching a Salesperson in a Deal Review

Ask for the client's last concrete move, the decision to be made, and what
could block it. Separate result (contract signed), intermediate objective
(budget validated), and activity the salesperson controls (prepare the budget
review with their contact). An email sent is an activity. A meeting accepted
by the decision-maker is a different signal. Never invent a close
probability. End the review on an observable action, its owner, and the date
the team decides to pursue or drop the deal from the forecast.
