## Core concepts

- **BATNA** (MESORE): what the user actually does if no deal. A prospect that
  has not answered is not a BATNA. Improve it *before* negotiating.
- **Reservation point**: the worst deal still better than the BATNA. Derived
  from numbers, not mood, and checked against the BATNA itself (a planning
  rate is not a walk-away point). Freelance cost floor:
  `(revenu minimum + charges) / jours facturables réalistes`; the reservation
  point is the higher of that floor and what the BATNA really yields. Use the
  user's own figures for billable days; many freelancers overestimate them.
- **Target**: ambitious and defensible with criteria the other side
  recognizes (market surveys the user can cite, comparable offers, cost of
  their alternative).
- **ZOPA**: overlap between both reservation points at equal scope. Adding
  on-call or scope changes the ZOPA: recompute.

## First offer and anchoring

| Situation | Move |
|---|---|
| User has solid data, other side has less | Anchor first: ambitious, precise, 2-3 criteria |
| User lacks information | Make them talk first: budget, constraints, grid |
| They anchored first and low | Do not counter at once. De-anchor ("on ne parle pas du même périmètre"), restate own criteria, then counter |
| Form asks "prétentions salariales" | Tandem range, low end = the ambitious point offer you would have made |

Rules:

- Precision must be real. A figure derived from a calculation the user can
  show (742 = cost floor + on-call premium) anchors better than a round one
  (Mason et al., 2013). Decorative precision is a small invented fact, and
  excessive precision can backfire with expert buyers (Loschelder et al.,
  2016). Round numbers are fine when nothing justifies the decimals.
- Tandem range (Ames & Mason, 2015): the low end is the ambitious point offer
  you would have made alone, the high end sits above it. A range whose low
  end is merely the target caps the outcome at the target; one that dips
  below target hands away the difference.
- Ambitious offers increase impasse risk (Petrowsky et al., 2025), so weigh
  how much the user needs this particular deal.

## Pricing the deal

Before any number, pin down: buyer or seller side, unit (day, sprint, fixed
price), currency, HT or TTC, firm duration, expenses, real workload, options,
and exit terms. Count as acquired only what is guaranteed; a likely renewal is
a separate scenario. A rate seen elsewhere is a reference point whose
comparability must be checked (same zone, same date, same terms). If the user
wants a current market rate, search dated comparables or mark `NEEDS-REVIEW`.

**Envelope vs unit cap.** « Les achats bloquent à 900 » can mean a per-day cap
or a total budget divided by planned days. Ask before countering:

> « Quand vous dites 900 €, c'est le maximum par journée facturée, ou
> l'enveloppe de la mission ramenée au nombre de jours prévu ? »

Total envelope → fewer days or narrower scope answers it. Per-day cap → move
other terms (travel paid, grouped on-site days, payment terms) or reach the
person who can grant an exception:

> « À ce tarif, est-ce que vous pouvez prendre les trajets en charge et
> regrouper les journées sur site ? »

**Compare offers on net value, same horizon.** Illustrative case, 10
guaranteed days. Offer A: 950 € HT/j, two trips at 180 € left to the user, so
9 140 € net of those costs. Offer B: 920 € HT/j, travel paid, so 9 200 €. B
wins by 60 €; covering those two trips takes 36 €/j more over 10 days. Travel
time, other costs, and payment delay still need comparing. With a cost range,
compute both bounds.

## Concession plans

**Ackerman (Voss-led register only).**

- Buyer (supplier purchase): target T. Offers at 65 %, 85 %, 95 %, 100 % of T.
- Seller (TJM, quote): the Black Swan Group study guide inverts it as roughly
  135 %, 115 %, 105 %, 100 % of T. The opening must stay credible for the
  market and scope; an absurd anchor costs credibility, not just money.
- Cap the opening at the top of what the user can justify for this scope and
  market. If 135 % of target is not defensible, open at the defensible
  ceiling and compress the steps.
- Decreasing steps. Last number non-round only if it comes from a real
  calculation. Add a non-monetary item at the end (payment terms, revision
  date) to signal the floor.
- Between steps: get a counter-offer from them, using labels and calibrated
  questions. Never move twice in a row without their move in between.
- Not for CDI salary, not with a manager: escalating counter-offers read as
  haggling.

**MESO (multiple equivalent simultaneous offers).** Two or three complete
packages of equal value to the user, different in composition:

- "780 €/j avec astreinte" (illustrative figures)
- "730 €/j sans astreinte"
- "forfait plafonné à N jours, changements de périmètre facturés à part"

Their choice reveals their priorities. Works well in writing. Call them
"equivalent" only after checking they are equivalent *for the user* (money,
time tied up, risk, preference). Then ask which element drove their choice.

A move is an exchange that can be verified: « Si les jours sur site sont
regroupés et les déplacements pris en charge, je peux revoir ma proposition. »
A concession promised against a vague intention stays provisional.

## When splitting the gap is fine

Splitting by reflex is anti-pattern N1. Splitting by decision is legitimate
when all of these hold:

- the midpoint is above the reservation point and beats the BATNA;
- the gap is small relative to what further rounds cost (time, goodwill,
  the next contract);
- the relationship is repeated and the counterpart has moved as much as the
  user;
- it closes the whole package, not one issue while others stay open.

Even then, make it conditional and closing: « Si on part sur 745 avec un
démarrage le 3, je signe aujourd'hui. » Say in the output that the split is a
choice, and why.

## Playbooks

### Salaire / augmentation (CDI)

- Register: interest-led. No Ackerman.
- Package, not salary alone: fixe, variable, prime d'arrivée, télétravail,
  RTT, formation et certifications, date de révision, titre, position et
  coefficient de convention collective.
- Budgets are often frozen at annual review cycles: ask *when* the budget is
  set and who decides, not only *how much*.
- Social backlash on asking is documented (Bowles, Babcock & Lai, 2007),
  notably for women. Relational framing reduces it (Bowles & Babcock, 2013):
  cite a real reason others recognise, e.g. « je veux que ça marche pour
  l'équipe aussi », or « mon manager m'a encouragé à en parler » *only if
  that happened*. Never invent the endorsement.
- Malhotra's rule: negotiate all issues together, not in slices.

### TJM freelance, ESN, portage

- Register: Voss-led toward procurement or a first deal with an ESN.
  Renewals with the same ESN are a repeated game: interest-led, keep
  calibrated questions, drop Ackerman.
- An ESN protects a margin: the client rate is not the consultant rate. Ask
  "Comment est construit le tarif côté client ?"
- Tradeables: rate, days, duration, renewal, remote, payment terms, deposit,
  on-call, revision or indexation clause, notice, travel costs,
  non-solicitation and non-compete clauses, reuse of tooling.
- Tie long commitment to terms: "12 mois si paiement à 30 jours et révision à
  6 mois."

### Scope creep

- Every out-of-scope request is currency: time, budget, or something removed.
- Calibrated question first: "Qu'est-ce qu'on sort du sprint pour l'intégrer ?"
- Then options (MESO): add with a costed amendment, swap with item X, defer.
- Write it down: amendment or documented trade-off, owner, date.

### Facture impayée

- Register: formal once the invoice is past due. Facts first: invoice number,
  amount, due date, requested payment date.
- Normative lever: cite their own terms and commitments (Diamond's
  "standards").
- French B2B late-payment rules (Code de commerce L441-10, D441-5) set
  payment deadlines, late penalties due by right, and a fixed recovery
  indemnity. State them only after checking the current text and the user's
  contract; mark `NEEDS-REVIEW` otherwise.
- Ladder: amicable reminder → formal notice (mise en demeure) → mediation →
  court procedure. Past the amicable stage, refer to a professional.
- A no-oriented question can soften stage one ("Est-ce que quelque chose
  bloque le règlement de votre côté ?"). Self-pity questions weaken a demand
  that can be stated directly.

### Désaccord technique avec un manager

- Register: interest-led. Banned: Ackerman, fake deadlines, negative leverage.
- Compare hypotheses, measurements, cost, rollback, and decision criteria.
- Propose a reversible test with an agreed stop criterion: "canary à 10 %,
  arrêt si le taux d'erreur dépasse le seuil convenu. Qui valide ce seuil ?"
- Label the concern before the argument: "On dirait que le risque principal
  pour toi, c'est la fenêtre de mise en prod."
- In meetings with senior management, prefer questions to frontal
  contradiction; settle the disagreement one-on-one, document it after
  (ADR, mail).

## Impasse and clean exit

Ask whether the refusal is final and what could reopen the topic, if the
conversation allows. If the BATNA is better, leave simply, without a bluff:

> « Merci pour la transparence. À ces conditions, je passe mon tour. Si le
> rythme de présence évolue, on pourra en reparler. »

An informed refusal is a good outcome. Walking away is the user's decision
(Core Rule 6), never the default recommendation.
